2-Week Low-Balsam, Propylene-Glycol-Free Diet

Pick any breakfast, lunch and dinner from the lists below, in any combination. The goal is two weeks with no balsam of Peru cross-reactors, no added flavorings and no propylene glycol, so that the night itch has a fair test.

The label rulePackaged food is only OK if the ingredient list is short and has no "flavor," "natural flavors," "spices" or "propylene glycol" (also listed as E1520 or 1,2-propanediol).
Season withSalt, black pepper, garlic, onion, chives, thyme, rosemary, oregano, sage, olive or avocado oil, butter, distilled white vinegar.
Eating outJapanese is the lowest risk, Thai is moderate, and Indian is the highest. Log every meal out so you can match it against the itch.
Keep the test cleanScore your itch each morning. Use free-and-clear detergent, no dryer sheets, and take your watch off at night.

Breakfast

  1. Oatmeal with bananaRolled oats, sliced banana, a pat of butter, a pinch of salt and sugar.
  2. Scrambled eggs and toastEggs with chives, plain bakery toast, butter.
  3. Yogurt bowlPlain Greek yogurt, diced pear, homemade plain granola (oats, nuts, butter, sugar, salt; no cinnamon or vanilla).
  4. Banana oat pancakesBlend 1 cup oats, 2 eggs and 1 ripe banana; cook in butter. Top with banana, not syrup.
  5. Spinach-cheddar omeletTwo eggs, spinach, onion, plain cheddar.
  6. Eggs and potatoesFried eggs, rosemary roast potatoes, sautéed spinach.
  7. Peanut butter toastPeanut butter (peanuts and salt only) and sliced banana on plain toast.
  8. Baked apple oatmealOats, milk, egg, grated apple, sugar and butter, baked in a dish on Sunday and reheated all week.
  9. Rice porridgeLeftover rice simmered in water or homemade stock, topped with a soft egg, chives and salt. No ginger.
  10. Diner breakfastOrder outEggs any style, plain toast, plain home fries.Say: "No seasoning or paprika on the potatoes, no ketchup, butter is fine."

Lunch

  1. Chicken rice bowlLeftover roast chicken, rice, cucumber, broccoli, oil and white vinegar dressing.
  2. Tuna saladCanned tuna (tuna, water, salt only), olive oil, white vinegar, celery, chives. Serve on greens or plain bread.
  3. Salad barOrder outGreens, cucumber, carrots, celery, broccoli, hard-boiled egg, chickpeas, plain cheese, plain seeds. Bring your own dressing.Skip their dressings, red wine or balsamic vinegar, marinated chicken, croutons, pickles, olives and tomatoes.
  4. Japanese lunchOrder outSashimi or simple rolls (salmon, tuna, cucumber, avocado), edamame, steamed rice.Say: "No sauce brushed on the fish, no spicy mayo or eel sauce." Skip the pickled ginger, wasabi and ponzu; use little or no soy sauce.
  5. Potato hashLeftover chicken or beef with diced potatoes, onion and spinach, topped with a fried egg.
  6. Lentil soupLentils, carrot, onion, celery and thyme in water or homemade stock, with plain bread.
  7. Chopped saladHard-boiled eggs, chickpeas, cucumber, carrots, cheddar, greens and house vinaigrette.
  8. Stuffed baked potatoLeftover beef or chicken, steamed broccoli, plain cheddar.
  9. Salmon quinoa saladLeftover salmon, quinoa, greens, cucumber, olive oil and white vinegar.
  10. Chicken salad sandwichChopped chicken, homemade mayo (egg yolk, avocado oil, white vinegar, salt; no lemon or mustard), celery, on plain bread.

Dinner

  1. Herb-roast chicken thighsWith potatoes and carrots on one sheet pan; rosemary, thyme, garlic.
  2. Pan-seared fishSalmon or cod, quinoa, garlic green beans.
  3. Garlic-sage pork chopsMashed potatoes, roast carrots.
  4. Beef and broccoli stir-fryGarlic, onion, salt and pepper over rice. No soy sauce or ginger.
  5. Garlic pastaPlain pasta, olive oil, garlic, zucchini, spinach, Parmesan.
  6. Burger nightPlain ground beef, salt and pepper, on plain buns with lettuce and onion; oven fries with salt and white vinegar.
  7. Oregano tofu or chickenRice, roast cauliflower.
  8. Japanese takeoutOrder out · lower riskSalt-grilled salmon or mackerel (shioyaki), rice, edamame, simple rolls.Say: "No lemon, no sauce on the fish." Skip the ginger, wasabi, ponzu, teriyaki and ginger salad dressing.
  9. Thai takeoutOrder out · moderate riskGarlic-pepper chicken or pork (gai or moo kratiem prik thai) with plain jasmine rice; or egg fried rice.Say: "No chili, no lime, no basil, no lemongrass, no ginger, no tomato." The fish, soy and oyster sauces can't be checked, so log it.
  10. Indian takeoutOrder out · highest riskPlain naan or roti, plain basmati rice, and chicken or paneer only if the kitchen will cook it with just salt, oil and garlic.Almost every curry, dal and tandoori dish contains ginger, garam masala (cinnamon, cloves), chili or tomato. If they can't do it plain, save Indian for after the trial.

  2. Fruit and proteinAn apple with plain peanut butter; melon with cheddar.
  3. NutsPlain almonds or cashews.
  4. YogurtPlain Greek yogurt with banana and a little sugar.
  5. Baked fruitBaked apple or pear with butter and sugar.

Alcohol

Drinking nothing for two weeks gives the cleanest test. Alcohol can make itch worse at night by flushing the skin, whatever is in the glass. If you do drink, stick to the "lowest risk" column, keep it occasional, and write down every drink in your itch log.

Mixers: tonic, cola, ginger beer or ale, juice, lime or lemon garnish 		Wine, beer, gin, vermouth, bitters and spiced liquors are on the standard low-balsam avoid list.

Aging in oak barrels creates vanillin and related compounds, which are relatives of balsam of Peru. That's why whiskey and bourbon taste of vanilla.

Gin's flavor comes from juniper and other botanicals, often including citrus peel.

Beer and many mixers can contain propylene glycol or "natural flavors" that aren't listed on the label.

Some tequilas contain small amounts of flavoring or sweetener that don't have to be disclosed, so look for brands that say "additive-free."

At a bar or restaurant: order vodka soda with no lime, or a 100% agave blanco tequila neat or with soda. Skip cocktails, since you can't check what goes into them. Barrel-aged spirits and wine are good things to test early when you start adding foods back.
