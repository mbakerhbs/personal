Step 1: diet trial

I plan a strict low-balsam, propylene-glycol-free diet starting Monday evening. I'm bringing 4 nights of baseline itch scores. I'm not sure I can keep up a very restrictive diet indefinitely, so I see it as a test, not a long-term fix.

  • Why I think it's worth testing: the night itch on my trunk and wrists comes hours after meals, which fits a delayed reaction to something I eat. In the main study, about half of patients allergic to balsam of Peru or fragrance mix improved on the diet (Salam & Fowler, 2001).
  • How long: I'd planned 2 weeks. The literature suggests at least 6 weeks (LearnSkin), so I'd extend if I see partial improvement.
  • Keeping it clean: I'll switch to a free-and-clear detergent, stop using dryer sheets, and take my watch off at night, because rubber accelerators and fragrance also touch my wrists and trunk.
  • Order of steps: I'd like to run the diet before adding a new cream, so I can tell which one helped. Unless you think the cream should come first.

Step 3: Dupixent or another biologic

I'm comfortable with self-injection. I think decades of steroid use without lasting control should be enough to justify it to insurance. Two issues need a plan before I start: my lymphoma history and my eyes.

Lymphoma. Studies have linked Dupixent to cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (CTCL).

  • A large records study found a relative risk of 4.59, highest in the first year and in adults (Mandel et al., 2024). In absolute terms, the rate was 0.28% vs 0.061%.
  • An FDA adverse-event analysis found the signal strongest in men aged 45 to 65 (Cabrera-Perez et al., JACI 2025).
  • Part of the signal may be CTCL that was already present and mistaken for eczema, then unmasked by the drug.
  • My lymphoma was B-cell, not T-cell, and I've been out of treatment for about 13 years. As far as I can tell, the Dupixent trials don't tell us much about people after a transplant.
  • What I'd like: your view on whether a skin biopsy is needed first (an earlier psoriasis workup was negative, and I don't have large uncontrolled scaly areas), and agreement from my oncologist.

Eyes. Dupixent commonly causes conjunctivitis, eye itch, blepharitis and dry eye (MHRA). I already have dry eye and blurry vision, and I use drops and a lid scrub inconsistently. Eye specialists suggest seeing an eye doctor before starting to get the eye surface in good shape first (Review of Optometry). I'd want an eye plan, and a commitment from me to stick to it, before the first injection.

Alternatives to ask about: the other injectable biologics for eczema (tralokinumab, lebrikizumab, nemolizumab). Do they carry less eye or lymphoma concern for someone like me? I'm assuming oral JAK pills are off the table, since the JAK class warning includes lymphoma.
